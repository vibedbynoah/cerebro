# Tune for this machine (processor settings), 2026-10-18

i9-12900H (6 P-cores with 2 threads, 8 E-cores; 20 logical), Windows 11, AC power, Ultimate
Performance plan. `examples/tune.rs` and the app's ignored test `tune_on_a_real_model_keeps_the_conversation`.
Engine 9e3aceb (method), 2674e7e (busy check excludes Cerebro's own threads).

Method (tune.rs): 5 s warm-up at the starting settings, then thread counts from the core layout,
work splitting {1,2,4,8} and four-row dots on/off, each timed as the median of 3 ~1 s repetitions in
forward/backward rounds; ties within 2% go to fewer threads or the current value; the winner is
kept only if a final alternating round shows >=3% faster, or as fast (within 1%) with fewer threads.

| Model, placement | Start (16 threads) | Tuned | Kept |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-0.6B Q8_0, CPU, run 1 | 46.6 tok/s | 48.8 tok/s, 12 threads (+4.7%) | yes |
| Qwen3-0.6B Q8_0, CPU, run 2 | 47.2 | 48.2, 12 threads (+2.2%, 4 fewer threads) | yes |
| Qwen3-14B Q4_K_M, split 31/40 | 9.7 | 9.7, 12 threads (4 fewer threads) | yes |

Both models settle on 12 threads: the six performance cores with both hyperthreads. The E-cores add
nothing to writing speed here and add heat.

Sustained speeds are ~20% under short bursts on this laptop (59 tok/s for a 128-token burst vs ~47
sustained), which is what the tuner measures. The first unordered version timed the first candidate
cold (53 tok/s on its first repetition, 37 on its third), hence the warm-up and alternating rounds.
